Aman runs the firm. He sets the thesis, makes the calls, and keeps the partnership pointed at companies worth a decade of work — the kind where capital and engineering both have something to do.

His bias is toward operators: founders who have shipped before, and problems that survive contact with a codebase. He treats the portfolio like a product — instrumented, reviewed, and improved in the open — and holds the firm to the same standard he holds founders.

He also owns the firm's relationships with its investors and partners, and treats their trust the way we treat a production deploy: carefully, and with a rollback plan.
