One of our portfolio apps was quietly losing orders. Not errors — delays. The billing webhooks were processed serially, and after their summer growth, the queue was measured in *days* while the dashboard showed a green checkmark.

**The bug** was the architecture, not the code: one background worker, one queue, all event types lumped together. Restore-progress looked fine; checkout did not.

**The fix, in three commits:**

- Split the queue by event type — checkout and cancellation get their own lanes with their own SLAs. Two hours.
- Add a dead-letter queue with alerting on retry depth instead of failure count. One afternoon.
- Backfill and replay from the provider's event log, then a two-week shadow mode where both pipelines ran and pivoted to the new one. This was the boring, load-bearing part.

**Total:** a long weekend of our engineers' time, no customer-visible incident. The founders did the product work; the plumbing came from us.

That's the whole deal of capital + code. The check is table stakes; the checkout lane is ours to unblock.
